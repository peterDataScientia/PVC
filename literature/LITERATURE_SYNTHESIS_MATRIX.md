# Literature Synthesis Matrix

## Thermal Degradation Kinetics and Lifetime Prediction of Perovskite Solar Cells

**Literature coverage:** foundational and recent experimental/computational studies (2022–2026). **Matrix format:** Study | Methodology | Key findings | Critical synthesis. The final column is an evidence-based appraisal, not a claim that the cited authors acknowledged the same limitation. Source datasets are listed separately below the literature matrix.

### Table 1. Critical literature synthesis of perovskite solar-cell stability and lifetime research

| Study | Methodology | Key findings | Critical synthesis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jacobsson et al. (2022)** — [Nature Energy](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-021-00941-3) | Systematic collection and curation of published perovskite photovoltaic device records; open database of **>42,400 devices** covering device structures, fabrication and performance variables. | Established a large resource for comparative analysis of device performance and materials/processing relationships. Coverage of degradation endpoints and conditions varies between reports. | **Enables scale but not automatically comparability.** Large device counts cannot replace matched ageing trajectories, consistent temperature conditions or validated lifetime endpoints. Our work uses temperature-resolved MPPT trajectories for kinetics rather than inferring kinetics from heterogeneous cross-sectional PCE records. |
| **Hartono et al. (2023)** — [Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-40585-3) | High-throughput operational ageing study using **2,245 MPPT trajectories**; standardization of curves to **150 h**, statistical comparisons, and **self-organizing map (SOM)** clustering of degradation shapes. [Original code](https://github.com/noortitan/PvkSOM). | Identified distinct temporal patterns of efficiency change and a statistical association between greater initial efficiency and short-term stability in the analyzed devices. | **Characterizes ageing patterns, but does not establish long-term lifetime transferability.** SOM groups describe curve shapes rather than uniquely identifying mechanisms. Public processed curves have limited per-curve experimental metadata and a 150-h window. Our study addresses kinetic-model assumptions and T80 forecast diagnostics rather than repeating unsupervised clustering. |
| **Alsulami et al. (2024)** — [Journal of Materials Chemistry A](https://doi.org/10.1039/D3TA05966A) | Data-driven PSC stability prediction using **6,038 records** and **SMOreg**; performance analyzed by architecture, including **p–i–n** device subsets. | Reported correlation of **0.963 for the p–i–n subset** and **0.699 for the pooled architectures** in the stated training results. | **Architecture matters for model behavior.** A strong training correlation is not independent validation; heterogeneous systems may confound an apparently general relation. Our work distinguishes within-trajectory, held-out-sample, and temperature-transfer tests and avoids treating pixel traces as independent devices. |
| **Dunlap-Shohl et al. (2024)** — [Journal of Materials Chemistry A](https://doi.org/10.1039/D3TA06668A) | Physicochemical features and machine learning applied to **45 degradation experiments** involving **CH₃NH₃PbI₃** devices, with prediction of **T80** from early-time information. | Demonstrated the possibility of using early ageing behavior for T80 prediction; the study reports an approximately **40% average predictive accuracy** under its own evaluation definition. | **Direct precedent for lifetime prediction, not a wholly unstudied topic.** Relatively small, material-specific evidence and the choice of T80 metric complicate extrapolation to other devices. Our question is whether alternative kinetic laws produce *reliable out-of-sample T80* for the available accelerated-ageing datasets; failure to do so must be reported. |
| **Flexible MAPbI₃ module thermal-stability study (2024)** — [Solar Energy Materials and Solar Cells](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.solmat.2024.113148) | Accelerated thermal exposure at **85, 95 and 105 °C** with periodic photovoltaic measurements; degradation-rate estimation and Arrhenius-based extrapolation. | Reported an **apparent activation energy of 1.062 eV** for maximum output and multi-stage behavior in some electrical-performance parameters. | **Shows Arrhenius analysis is established, but its parameter is context-specific.** Activation energies estimated from different stacks, temperature ranges, operational modes and endpoints should not be compared as though they measure one universal mechanism. Our study tests sensitivity to rate definition, observation window and sample grouping. |
| **Self-assembled bilayer stability study (2025 volume)** — [Nature Energy](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-024-01689-2) | Experimental comparison of perovskite devices incorporating conventional self-assembled monolayers and a stabilized bilayer interface, including elevated-temperature stress measurements. | Reported improved resistance to thermally accelerated performance loss for the modified interfacial architecture. | **Thermal ageing depends on material and interface design.** Pooling SAM and non-SAM systems into a single activation-energy estimate without assessing architecture effects would weaken interpretation. Dataset-specific kinetics and inter-dataset comparison should be kept separate unless protocols are compatible. |

### Table 2. Cross-study critical synthesis

| Evidence theme | What the literature establishes | What remains unresolved for our data | Implication for this study |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Temperature-dependent degradation** | Experimental heat stress and high-throughput MPPT measurements show that degradation dynamics depend on temperature and device conditions. | The stability of estimates when temperature groups have markedly different monitoring durations and start-up behavior. | Compare normalized PCE and apparent rates using explicit, sensitivity-tested windows and device/sample grouping. |
| **Arrhenius kinetics** | Apparent Arrhenius parameters have been extracted in defined accelerated-ageing systems. | Whether a single temperature dependence remains adequate across the studied ranges and kinetic phases. | Evaluate apparent activation energy and goodness of fit; do not equate it with one verified molecular mechanism. |
| **Kinetic-model comparison** | Both empirical and physically informed descriptions are established. | Whether additional flexibility improves *independent-sample prediction* rather than only fit to observed trajectories. | Distinguish temporal holdout from leave-sample-out validation; report uncertainty and inconclusive comparisons. |
| **T80 endpoint and lifetime forecasting** | Published studies have investigated early-life prediction of T80. | How often the available curves yield genuine sustained future T80 events, and whether predictions match those events. | Account for recovery, measurement gaps, left/interval/right censoring, and non-estimable forecasts; do not claim accurate lifetime prediction unsupported by tests. |
| **Transportability between device systems** | Composition, interface, architecture and stress protocol influence stability. | Whether kinetic estimates from different accelerated-ageing archives describe commensurable stress/measurement conditions. | Analyze archives separately first; treat pooling and outdoor extrapolation as hypotheses requiring evidence. |

### Evidence-based research gap

Previous studies establish that (i) perovskite stability varies with device conditions, (ii) time-resolved ageing curves reveal heterogeneous patterns, (iii) apparent Arrhenius rates are used in accelerated tests, and (iv) T80 prediction is possible in some specific study designs. The present study therefore **does not claim that Arrhenius analysis, degradation modeling or T80 prediction are new in themselves**. Its focused contribution is the critical test of **how model choice, sample hierarchy, temperature window and threshold definition influence measured kinetic estimates and out-of-sample lifetime predictability** in two publicly available accelerated-ageing archives. Conclusions are bounded by what the validation results support.

**Reporting boundary:** Our previously obtained grouped-validation results did not establish broadly reliable T80 prediction or robust superiority of the stretched-exponential model. Those are reported as methodological findings and limitations, not converted into positive claims.
