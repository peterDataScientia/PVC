# Literature Synthesis Matrix

## Thermal Degradation Kinetics and Lifetime Prediction of Perovskite Solar Cells

**Scope:** Experimental and computational evidence on photovoltaic ageing, temperature-dependent kinetics, and lifetime prediction. The matrix distinguishes what each study measured from what its results allow us to infer. Critical synthesis follows **evidence → scientific interpretation → cross-study implication → evidence boundary**, without presenting research opportunities as established findings.

### Table 1. Critical literature synthesis

| Study | Methodology | Key findings | Critical synthesis and translational interpretation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Jacobsson et al. (2022)** — [Nature Energy](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-021-00941-3) | Systematic compilation of **more than 42,400 published perovskite devices**, recording device composition, architecture, fabrication conditions, performance, and available stability information. | Established a large, structured resource for studying relationships between material/device characteristics and reported photovoltaic performance. Reporting detail and stability endpoints vary across primary studies. | The database moves photovoltaic research beyond isolated record efficiencies toward evidence drawn from many devices. **However, breadth is not equivalent to kinetic comparability:** a performance value measured under one experimental protocol cannot be treated as a point on the same degradation trajectory as a value measured under another. Consequently, temperature-dependent lifetime analysis requires time-resolved measurements with traceable test conditions; the number of literature records alone cannot establish a transferable degradation law. |
| **Hartono et al. (2023)** — [Nature Communications](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-023-40585-3); [analysis repository](https://github.com/noortitan/PvkSOM) | Analyzed **2,245 high-throughput MPPT ageing curves** standardized to approximately **150 h**, using statistical analysis and self-organizing maps to examine degradation patterns and their relationship with initial device efficiency. | Reported heterogeneous ageing-curve patterns and an association between higher initial performance and better short-term stability in the investigated devices. | The important advance is the recognition that operational stability is not represented by a single uniform decay shape. **Yet the ability to classify an observed trajectory is different from the ability to forecast its future threshold crossing.** In particular, a standardized 150-h observation window may describe early instability without establishing long-term T80. This distinction makes independent prediction testing necessary before a descriptive ageing pattern is promoted to a lifetime estimate. |
| **Alsulami et al. (2024)** — [Journal of Materials Chemistry A](https://doi.org/10.1039/D3TA05966A) | Examined data-driven perovskite stability modeling across **6,038 records** using sequential minimal optimization regression (SMOreg), including device-architecture-specific analyses. | Reported correlations of **0.963 in a p–i–n subset** and **0.699 in a pooled architecture analysis** for the specified training evaluations. | The contrast indicates that mixing device architectures may obscure relationships that appear more consistent within a more homogeneous group. **However, a higher training correlation does not establish that the model will predict an unseen sample more accurately.** The practical implication is that stability models should be evaluated at the level of independent samples and device systems, rather than assigning all pixel-level traces to unrelated training and test sets. This is a generalizability question, not proof of a particular degradation mechanism. |
| **Dunlap-Shohl et al. (2024)** — [Journal of Materials Chemistry A](https://doi.org/10.1039/D3TA06668A) | Combined physicochemical degradation information and machine-learning methods to predict **T80** from **45 ageing experiments** involving **CH₃NH₃PbI₃-based devices**. | Demonstrated a study-specific approach to predicting the time at which device performance reaches 80% of its reference value; the publication discusses approximately **40%** average predictive accuracy under its reported metric. | This work establishes that early degradation measurements can contain information relevant to a future lifetime endpoint. **It does not follow that any well-fitted early curve yields a reliable T80 prediction.** Predictive performance remains conditional on the tested material system, endpoint definition, time horizon, and validation procedure. Therefore, a rigorous assessment must distinguish a forecast made before the crossing from a retrospective fit to measurements that already contain the event. |
| **Flexible MAPbI₃ module thermal-stability study (2024)** — [Solar Energy Materials and Solar Cells](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.solmat.2024.113148) | Evaluated accelerated thermal ageing of flexible modules at **85, 95 and 105 °C**, using electrical-performance measurements and Arrhenius-type analysis. | Reported an **apparent activation energy of 1.062 eV** for maximum-output degradation, while other performance parameters exhibited nonuniform or staged responses. | Temperature-dependent acceleration is physically informative because it links changes in a measurable degradation rate to thermal stress. **Nevertheless, the fitted activation energy describes the chosen device system, observable, and temperature range; it does not, by itself, identify a unique microscopic process.** Divergent behaviors among electrical endpoints also warn against assuming that one rate constant captures all aspects of photovoltaic failure. Comparisons across studies must preserve the endpoint and experimental context. |
| **Self-assembled bilayer stability study (2025 volume)** — [Nature Energy](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41560-024-01689-2) | Investigated perovskite solar cells using self-assembled interfacial layers and compared thermal robustness across interface designs under accelerated stress conditions. | Reported improved stability for devices with the modified interface, demonstrating that engineering the interfacial structure can change the observed response to thermal stress. | The result connects materials design to operational durability: **thermal stability is not simply a function of temperature but also of the structure that experiences that temperature.** Consequently, an activation energy derived from one architecture should not automatically be transferred to another, even when the devices are measured under nominally similar conditions. This establishes an experimental rationale for analyzing distinct ageing archives separately before considering pooled lifetime models. |

### Table 2. Cross-study synthesis: what is established and what remains uncertain

| Research theme | Synthesis of existing evidence | Scientific implication and evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Heterogeneity of operational ageing** | Hartono et al. showed distinct short-term ageing trajectories, while the interfacial stability study demonstrated that material architecture alters resistance to thermal stress. | Degradation cannot be assumed to follow one universal trajectory. A model that fits pooled measurements may conceal architecture- or sample-specific behavior; observed curve differences alone do not prove a specific microscopic mechanism. |
| **Temperature-dependent apparent kinetics** | The flexible-module study applied Arrhenius analysis under controlled high-temperature stress. Reports of staged electrical responses indicate that estimated rates depend on the chosen observable and ageing interval. | Apparent activation energy can summarize temperature dependence **under defined assumptions**, but its value should not be interpreted as a material constant without validating the kinetic regime, rate definition, and comparison window. |
| **Prediction versus description** | Hartono et al. characterized ageing patterns; Dunlap-Shohl et al. explicitly examined T80 prediction. These are related but different scientific tasks. | A model may reproduce measured PCE trajectories without predicting future T80 accurately. Independent sample-group evaluation, forecast origin, and observed versus censored events must be made explicit. |
| **Apparent model accuracy and transferability** | Architecture-specific prediction results in Alsulami et al. differed from pooled-data results, while the large database described by Jacobsson et al. contains heterogeneous experimental evidence. | Strong fit statistics are not sufficient evidence of out-of-sample generalization. Validation must respect sample/pixel hierarchy and study-level differences; cross-temperature transfer requires a test that actually holds out a temperature group. |
| **Operational relevance of lifetime estimates** | Accelerated-test analysis quantifies performance changes within selected stress protocols, and early-life methods attempt to convert those measurements into practical lifetime metrics. | Extrapolated T80 should be reported as a model-dependent estimate, not as an observed operational lifetime. Claims about outdoor service life require comparable outdoor validation, which is not established by the two accelerated-ageing archives alone. |

### Table 3. Research-question alignment and dataset provenance

| Research question | Relevant evidence | What may be concluded from the available data |
|---|---|---|
| How does temperature influence measured degradation? | [2024 accelerated ageing data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.10902908); [2026 SAM accelerated ageing data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21242242) | Temperature-associated retention and apparent-rate differences **within specified devices and observation windows** |
| Does an Arrhenius-type law adequately describe apparent kinetic rates? | Same temperature-resolved archives, read alongside the 2024 flexible-module study | Estimated apparent activation energies and model adequacy, subject to temperature coverage, sample clustering, and rate-definition uncertainty |

### Integrative research rationale

Taken together, these studies demonstrate that perovskite degradation is shaped by experimental conditions and device architecture, that temporal ageing behavior can be heterogeneous, and that both Arrhenius-type kinetics and early-life T80 prediction have established precedents. **The unresolved issue is therefore not whether degradation can be fitted or whether T80 can ever be predicted. It is whether a fitted temperature-dependent description remains informative when applied to a future observation or an independent experimental sample.** The distinction is consequential: a plausible kinetic curve may offer a convincing retrospective explanation while failing to estimate when performance will cross a practically relevant threshold.

The present project examines this distinction using two temperature-resolved experimental ageing archives. Its interpretable contribution lies in testing how kinetic formulation, observation window, hierarchical sampling, and T80 definition affect rate estimates and prediction errors. Where out-of-sample performance is inadequate, that result will be reported directly rather than reframed as reliable lifetime prediction. This approach links laboratory ageing measurements to the practical question of what can—and cannot—be inferred about photovoltaic durability from accelerated tests.
